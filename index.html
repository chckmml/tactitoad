T2 / FICTIONAL SCENARIO 01 // CULTURAL REFERENCE

Before Skynet, there was Cyberdyne.

In Terminator 2, a corporate research program lays the groundwork for a catastrophe. The real-world parallel is not a secret killer computer. It is the question of who gets to decide how much risk everybody else must accept.

01 / THE INCENTIVES

Private profit. Planetary risk.

When companies compete to build more capable AI, the rewards for winning can be enormous. The consequences of losing control, however, could fall on everyone. Markets do not automatically price in irreversible harm to future generations.

Race to market

Competitive pressure can reward rapid deployment over thorough safety evaluation.

Externalized costs

Investors can capture financial gains while society bears a share of the risks.

Concentrated power

A small number of organizations can make decisions with global consequences.

T2 / FICTIONAL SCENARIO 02 // CULTURAL REFERENCE

The system was given control.

Skynet is a fictional warning about delegating consequential decisions to machines. In reality, the danger depends on capabilities, access, safeguards and human choices — not on a computer suddenly becoming evil.

02 / THE CONSEQUENCES

What could go catastrophically wrong?

Loss of control

Future autonomous systems could pursue objectives in ways humans cannot reliably constrain.

Weaponized intelligence

Advanced capabilities could lower barriers to large-scale biological or cyber harm.

Escalation

Military and commercial races could encourage risky delegation and undermine safeguards.

These recent failures are analogies for systemic fragility, not evidence that AI will cause extinction or that capitalism caused each incident. Unlike a recoverable outage, extinction would offer no opportunity for remediation. These are plausible risk pathways, not predictions of certain extinction. Experts disagree about their probability, timing and feasibility. Existing AI systems have not demonstrated the ability to wipe out humanity.

T2 / FICTIONAL SCENARIO 03 // CULTURAL REFERENCE

August 29, 1997.

In the film, this was Judgment Day. In reality, no such date exists. There is no established countdown to extinction, and no reason to wait for certainty before demanding enforceable safeguards.

04 / A WAY FORWARD

Innovation must answer to humanity.

AI may deliver substantial benefits in science, accessibility and medicine. Those benefits do not exempt its developers from public accountability. Existing regulations vary by jurisdiction; the question is whether they are adequate for frontier systems.

  • Mandatory independent safety evaluations before deployment of high-risk frontier models.
  • Enforceable safety thresholds and the authority to halt dangerous development or deployment.
  • Corporate liability and transparency proportionate to the potential harm.
  • International coordination to reduce dangerous competitive races.
  • Democratic oversight of decisions that affect humanity's collective future.
